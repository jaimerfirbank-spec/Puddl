Before you draw

Clear letters. Clear connections.

A few simple habits make your map easier to read. Use the examples below, then check the reading before creating your editable Puddle map.

1. Write each name clearly

  • Use separate CAPITAL letters where possible, with a small gap between letters and a larger gap between words.
  • Give straight letters clear corners. An L needs a vertical stroke and a horizontal foot that meet at a right angle.
  • Keep the natural curves in letters such as C, O, S and U. Make each letter recognisable, with clear openings and endings.
  • Use dark ink on plain, light paper. Keep names comfortably inside their shapes, clear of borders and connecting lines.
  • Keep the person's actual spelling, including accents and punctuation. If a name is long, use two lines inside a larger shape.

L and C

Make the L corner square. Leave the right side of C open.

I and J

Give I a straight stem. Make the bottom hook on J visible.

U and V

Use a rounded bottom for U and a distinct point for V.

O and zero

Close the O. If you use the number 0, a slash helps distinguish it.

Example: LOUIS. The square corner on the L helps it read as L rather than C. Keep the letters separate.

2. Draw the connections

  • Give every node a clear, closed outline.
  • Run each line from one node's border to the other node's border.
  • For a one-way connection, put a clear arrowhead at the receiving node.
  • For a two-way connection, draw an arrowhead at both ends.
  • Where lines cross, keep each line easy to trace and arrowheads clear of the crossing.
  • Leave deliberately unconnected nodes as they are.

3. Photograph the whole map

  • Keep every node and arrowhead in the frame.
  • Hold the camera as square to the page as practical.
  • Use even light and check for blur, glare or deep shadows.
  • Make sure the smallest names are readable when you zoom in on the photo.
Before creating the map: check names, arrow directions and any highlighted uncertainty. You can correct a reading without taking another photo.
